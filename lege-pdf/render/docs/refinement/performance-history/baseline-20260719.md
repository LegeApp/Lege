# Profiling baseline — 2026-07-19

This is the pre-optimization baseline for sections 1–4 of
`optimization-plan.md`. No hotspot optimization is included in this work.

## Environment

- Commit: `d5a1fc8` with a dirty worktree containing the profiling work.
- Compiler: `rustc 1.95.0 (59807616e 2026-04-14)`, LLVM 22.1.2.
- CPU: 13th Gen Intel Core i7-13700H, 20 logical CPUs.
- Build: release optimization with line-table debug information.
- Sampling/counters: forced frame pointers; performance-core PMU on this
  hybrid CPU.
- Structured sample counts: cold 3, warm 5, and class-dependent compiled and
  prepared counts from `pages.tsv`.
- DHAT scope: document open, one page compile, and one compiled-page render.

The permanent corpus is described by `manifest.json` and the runnable
`pages.tsv`. It contains 13 entries covering JPEG at viewer and sweep scale,
single-JPX scan, JPX/JBIG2 MRC, pure CCITT, Latin text, CJK CID text, Type 1,
real-world vector content, transparency, soft mask, tiling, and shading. The
high-resolution JPEG page is also the unusually slow real-world page.

All classes compiled and rendered successfully. Every output hash was stable
across cold, warm, compiled, and prepared modes.

## Stage attribution

These are compiled-page medians. Decode is included in lowering. Named
lowering, surface, execution, and output stages cover 99.47–100% of compiled
wall time. Document open, compilation, and render stages cover 95.84–100% of

The scale comparison is important: JPEG decode stays near 170 ms at both
sizes, while execution grows from 388 ms to 1622 ms. The high-resolution
deficit therefore grows in renderer-side destination-pixel work, not decoding.
Conversely, JPX decoding is material on the selected minified scan. MRC is
almost exactly split between JPX/JBIG2 decode and prepared execution.

Prepared execution removes little from CCITT, CJK, soft-mask, tiling, and
transparency pages, showing their cost is already in execution/output. It
removes 93% of the Type 1 page, making repeated font/glyph preparation a
separate measured problem. These are observations, not optimization choices.

Notable call-site evidence:

- The JPEG sweep holds one 162.62 MiB RGBA surface and one 162.62 MiB final
  host buffer concurrently. The structured model now captures this accurately.
- JPX allocates large planar component and Tier-1 coefficient buffers inside
  `jp2lam`; these explain the 149.8 MiB unmodeled peak.
- MRC has the same JPX intermediate pattern and is undercounted by 137.6 MiB.
- Latin text allocates 8 MiB across 18 outline-point growth events and has
  thousands of small CFF outline/verb allocations.
- Type 1 has the largest allocation count: 43,634. DHAT attributes thousands
  of small allocations to font-program/outline paths.
- Tiling performs 1,740 allocations totaling 1.77 MiB at its dominant
  execution call site despite its modest peak.
